## What are we talking about?

Anticipating means looking a little further than the next metre. A car door that might open, a ball rolling towards the road, a cyclist glancing over their shoulder: each of these clues tells you what is likely to happen next.

People who anticipate are not more careful out of virtue. They are simply **surprised less often**, and one surprise fewer means one sudden brake, one horn or one fright fewer.

## Why it is worth it for you

- You keep the initiative instead of reacting in a hurry.
- You move more smoothly, which is often more pleasant.
- You give others time to understand what you are about to do.

## A few simple habits

1. **Look far, then near**: scan the scene before focusing on the immediate obstacle.
2. **Spot the clues**: turning wheels, glances, indicators, children near a crossing.
3. **Keep a margin**: of distance, speed and attention, so you still have options when it matters.
4. **Make your intentions readable**: a hand signal, an indicator or a clear line help others anticipate in turn.

## What anticipation does not replace

Anticipating does not make up for a badly designed junction or an interrupted cycle lane. It is an individual lever, useful right away, that goes hand in hand with better infrastructure.
